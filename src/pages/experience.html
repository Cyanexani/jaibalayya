01 — Start screen

Your world.
At a glance.

Start is not a page of shortcuts. It is the surface your phone actually lives on: weather that changes, a calendar that moves on, unread counts that climb, photos that turn over, the track that is playing right now.

Every tile comes in three sizes. Press and hold to resize, drag to rearrange, unpin anything you do not use. The grid is yours — and it is the first thing you see.

02 — App list

Everything.
One swipe away.

Swipe left from Start and the whole system is there, in one alphabetical column. No folders to maintain, no pages to remember, no grid of identical squares to scan.

Tap a letter heading to jump the alphabet. Start typing to filter. The list is long by design — and that is fine, because you never have to read it.

03 — Live Tiles

Information
in motion.

A tile is a window into an app, not a link to one. The tile engine wakes, renders and sleeps on a shared schedule, so a Start screen full of live information costs a fraction of a percent of your battery.

04 — People

closer to what
matters.

Contacts from every account are merged into one person, once. Their calls, messages and photos live on the same card, and you can pin them straight to Start — so reaching somebody is a tile, not a search.

11 — Search

Search
beyond
the obvious.

The search key is a system service, not a web box. Apps, people, files, photos, settings and the web resolve in one ranked list — and the ranking learns from what you actually open.

Nothing you type is sent anywhere until you choose a web result.
